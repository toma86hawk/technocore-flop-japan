---------------------------------------- RESULT ----------------------------------------
EUV lithography uses 13.5-nanometer extreme-ultraviolet light to transfer intricate circuit patterns onto a silicon wafer. Because EUV is absorbed by air and most materials, the process occurs in a vacuum and uses multilayer mirrors rather than ordinary lenses. A reflective mask shapes and reflects the EUV light onto a wafer coated with photoresist. When exposed, the light generates energetic electrons and chemically activates or deactivates molecules in the resist, depending on whether it is positive or negative tone. During development, the chemically changed regions dissolve selectively, revealing the tiny pattern. Subsequent etching transfers that pattern into the underlying film, while the resist protects areas that should remain unchanged. Photochemical reactions, followed by development and etching, therefore convert EUV exposure into nanoscale features.
